Tyrannosaurus rex

Height: 3.7 to 4 meters

Length: 12 to 13 meters

Mass: 7 to 8 tons

Diet: Carnivore

Period: Late Cretaceous period (68 to 66 million years ago)

Tyrannosaurus rex (T. rex) was a massive, bipedal carnivore and apex predator that lived approximately 68 to 66 million years ago during the Late Cretaceous period in western North America. Physical Characteristics
  • Name Meaning: "Tyrant lizard king" (Tyrannosaurus from Greek for tyrant lizard, rex from Latin for king).
  • Size: Reached lengths of 40 to 43 feet (12.3–13 meters) and stood about 12 to 13 feet tall at the hip.
  • Weight: Estimated between 5.4 and 9.2 tons (approx. 5,400 to 9,200 kg).
  • Skull and Jaws: Possessed a heavy, massive skull measuring up to 5 feet (1.5 meters) long, driven by the most powerful bite force of any known terrestrial animal.
  • Teeth: Lined with about 60 serrated, conical teeth that could grow up to 6 to 12 inches (15–30 cm) long, including the root.
  • Limbs: Equipped with powerful, muscular three-toed hind legs and famously reduced, short forelimbs ending in two clawed digits.
  • Tail: A long, heavy tail balanced the massive head and torso.
Habitat and Diet
  • Discovery: Named in 1905 by Henry Fairfield Osborn after early fossil discoveries by collectors like Barnum Brown and Arthur Lakes.
  • Diet: A strict carnivore that hunted large herbivorous dinosaurs like hadrosaurs (Edmontosaurus), ceratopsians (Triceratops), and ankylosaurs, while also opportunistically scavenging.
  • Senses: Featured acute senses of smell and strong binocular vision.
History and Famous Fossils
  • Range: Inhabited lush, humid floodplains and forested environments in western North America (such as modern-day Montana, Wyoming, and parts of Canada).
  • Famous Specimens: Sue (housed at the Field Museum of Natural History) is one of the largest and most complete skeletons ever found.
  • Extinction: Vanished in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago, triggered largely by an asteroid impact.
