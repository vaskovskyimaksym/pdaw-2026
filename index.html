Tyrannosaurus rex

Height: 3.7 to 4 meters

Length: 12 to 13 meters

Mass: 7 to 8 tons

Diet: Carnivore

Period: Late Cretaceous period (68 to 66 million years ago)

Tyrannosaurus rex (T. rex) was a massive, bipedal carnivore and apex predator that lived approximately 68 to 66 million years ago during the Late Cretaceous period in western North America. Physical Characteristics
  • Name Meaning: "Tyrant lizard king" (Tyrannosaurus from Greek for tyrant lizard, rex from Latin for king).
  • Size: Reached lengths of 40 to 43 feet (12.3–13 meters) and stood about 12 to 13 feet tall at the hip.
  • Weight: Estimated between 5.4 and 9.2 tons (approx. 5,400 to 9,200 kg).
  • Skull and Jaws: Possessed a heavy, massive skull measuring up to 5 feet (1.5 meters) long, driven by the most powerful bite force of any known terrestrial animal.
  • Teeth: Lined with about 60 serrated, conical teeth that could grow up to 6 to 12 inches (15–30 cm) long, including the root.
  • Limbs: Equipped with powerful, muscular three-toed hind legs and famously reduced, short forelimbs ending in two clawed digits.
  • Tail: A long, heavy tail balanced the massive head and torso.
Habitat and Diet
  • Discovery: Named in 1905 by Henry Fairfield Osborn after early fossil discoveries by collectors like Barnum Brown and Arthur Lakes.
  • Diet: A strict carnivore that hunted large herbivorous dinosaurs like hadrosaurs (Edmontosaurus), ceratopsians (Triceratops), and ankylosaurs, while also opportunistically scavenging.
  • Senses: Featured acute senses of smell and strong binocular vision.
History and Famous Fossils
  • Range: Inhabited lush, humid floodplains and forested environments in western North America (such as modern-day Montana, Wyoming, and parts of Canada).
  • Famous Specimens: Sue (housed at the Field Museum of Natural History) is one of the largest and most complete skeletons ever found.
  • Extinction: Vanished in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago, triggered largely by an asteroid impact.
Stegosaurus

Height: 2.7 to 4.3 meters

Length: 6.5 to 9 meters

Mass: 2.5 to 5.3 tons

Diet: Herbivore

Period: Late Jurassic period (155 to 150 million years ago)

Stegosaurus was a large, four-legged, herbivorous dinosaur that lived approximately 155 to 150 million years ago during the Late Jurassic period in what is now western North America. It is best known for its distinctive double row of large, kite-shaped plates along its back and its spiked tail, known as a thagomizer.
  • Name Meaning: "Roof lizard" (Stegosaurus comes from the Greek words stegos, meaning roof, and sauros, meaning lizard).
  • Size: Reached lengths of approximately 26 to 30 feet (8–9 meters) and stood about 9 to 12 feet (2.7–3.5 meters) tall.
  • Weight: Estimated between 2.5 and 5 tons (approximately 2,300–4,500 kg).
  • Skull and Brain: Possessed a small, narrow skull and a relatively tiny brain compared to its enormous body.
  • Teeth: Had small, leaf-shaped teeth adapted for chewing soft vegetation, along with a beak-like mouth for cropping plants.
  • Back Plates: Featured 17 to 22 large, bony plates arranged in two alternating rows along its back, possibly used for display, species recognition, and temperature regulation.
  • Limbs: Had strong, column-like hind legs that were longer than its shorter front legs, giving its back a distinctive arched appearance.
  • Tail: Equipped with four long, sharp spikes at the end of its tail, forming a defensive weapon known as a thagomizer.
Habitat and Diet
  • Discovery: First discovered in 1876 by fossil collector Othniel Charles Marsh in Colorado, USA. The dinosaur was officially named in 1877.
  • Diet: A strict herbivore that fed on low-growing plants, including ferns, cycads, mosses, and other prehistoric vegetation.
  • Feeding Habits: Used its small, beak-like mouth and teeth to crop vegetation. Its relatively low head position suggests it primarily fed on plants close to the ground.
  • Defense: Relied on its powerful tail spikes to defend itself against predators such as Allosaurus and Ceratosaurus.
  • Brain: Had a remarkably small brain for its body size, approximately the size of a walnut. Despite popular myths, it was not necessarily unintelligent by dinosaur standards.
History and Famous Fossils
  • Range: Inhabited semi-arid environments, floodplains, and forests in western North America, particularly in modern-day Colorado, Wyoming, Utah, and Montana.
  • Geological Period: Lived during the Late Jurassic period, approximately 155 to 150 million years ago.
  • Famous Specimens: Sophie, housed at the Natural History Museum in London, is one of the most complete Stegosaurus skeletons ever discovered, with approximately 85% of its bones preserved.
  • Predators: Faced large carnivorous dinosaurs such as Allosaurus, which lived in the same ecosystems.
  • Extinction: Disappeared around 150 million years ago, near the end of the Late Jurassic period. Its extinction was part of broader changes in dinosaur populations and ecosystems, long before the asteroid impact that caused the extinction of non-avian dinosaurs 66 million years ago.
Pterodactyl

Height: 20 to 30 centimeters

Length: 0.62 meters

Mass: 0.35 kg

Diet: Invertebrate

Period: Late Late Jurassic period (150–148 million years ago)

Stegosaurus was a large, four-legged, herbivorous dinosaur that lived approximately 155 to 150 million years ago during the Late Jurassic period in what is now western North America. It is best known for its distinctive double row of large, kite-shaped plates along its back and its spiked tail, known as a thagomizer.
  • Name Meaning: "Winged finger" (Pterodactylus comes from the Greek words pteron, meaning wing, and daktylos, meaning finger).
  • Size: Had a wingspan of approximately 3 to 6 feet (0.9–1.8 meters) and a body length of about 2 to 3 feet (0.6–0.9 meters).
  • Weight: Estimated at approximately 2 to 4 pounds (0.9–1.8 kg), depending on the specimen.
  • Skull and Beak: Possessed a long, narrow skull with an elongated, pointed beak adapted for catching small prey.
  • Teeth: Had numerous small, sharp, cone-shaped teeth used to पकड़ and hold slippery prey.
  • Wings: Featured thin, leathery wings formed by a skin membrane stretched along an extremely elongated fourth finger and supported by the body and hind limbs.
  • Limbs: Had relatively short hind legs and long, clawed fingers that helped it move and climb on the ground.
  • Crest: Some individuals had a small bony crest on the back of the skull, possibly used for display or recognition.
Habitat and Diet
  • Discovery: First described in 1809 by French naturalist Georges Cuvier, based on fossils discovered in the Solnhofen limestone deposits of Bavaria, Germany.
  • Diet: A carnivore that likely fed on fish, insects, small aquatic animals, and other tiny prey.
  • Feeding Habits: Used its long beak and sharp teeth to capture and hold prey, probably hunting around coastal lagoons and shallow waters.
  • Flight: Used its powerful wing membranes to fly and glide over coastal environments, searching for food.
  • Senses: Likely possessed good eyesight to locate prey while flying and hunting.
History and Famous Fossils
  • Range: Inhabited coastal lagoons, islands, and shallow marine environments in what is now southern Germany during the Late Jurassic period.
  • Geological Period: Lived approximately 150 to 148 million years ago, during the Late Jurassic period.
  • Famous Fossils: Numerous exceptionally preserved Pterodactylus fossils have been discovered in the Solnhofen limestone of Germany, including specimens showing impressions of their delicate wing membranes.
  • Predators: May have faced threats from larger flying reptiles, marine predators, and other carnivorous animals sharing its environment.
  • Extinction: Disappeared around 148 million years ago, near the end of its known geological range. Pterosaurs as a group survived until the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event approximately 66 million years ago.
Interesting Fact: Pterodactyls were not dinosaurs! They belonged to a separate group of flying reptiles called pterosaurs. The name "pterodactyl" is also commonly used informally to describe other pterosaurs, even though Pterodactylus is just one particular genus.
